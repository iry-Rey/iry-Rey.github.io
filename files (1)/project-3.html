Buck Down Converter PCB Design

← All projects

Summary

Designed, built and tested a step-down (buck) converter on a custom PCB as an introduction to the full PCB workflow, from schematic and layout through assembly and bench testing. Built around the MC34063A switching regulator and co-designed as part of a lab group.

Author & developer·2026
  • KiCad
  • PCB Design
  • Power electronics
  • Oscilloscope Analysis

Design

  • Selected components from the MC34063A datasheet: a feedback divider to set the output voltage, three 1 Ω resistors in parallel (about 0.33 Ω) for current sensing, a 470 pF timing capacitor, 100 µF and 470 µF filter capacitors, an RCH8011NP-680L through-hole inductor, and an SB1100 Schottky catch diode chosen for its low forward drop and fast switching.
  • Drew the schematic and routed a two-layer board in KiCad, following the manufacturer’s evaluation-board layout.

    • Layout Considerations

  • Kept the high-frequency switching loop (input capacitor, internal switch and catch diode) as small as possible to limit parasitic inductance, ringing and EMI.
  • Used wide traces on the high-current paths (Vin, switching node, Vout) to reduce resistance and voltage spikes.
  • Added ground pours on both layers as a low-impedance reference and heatsink.
  • Routed the sensitive feedback trace away from the inductor’s magnetic field.

    • Outcome

    Skills gained: hands-on experience with PCB design in KiCad, switching-regulator component selection, layout for EMI and thermal performance, soldering and assembly, and oscilloscope testing/verification.
