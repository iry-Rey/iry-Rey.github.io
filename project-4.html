Superheterodyne Reciever Simulation

← All projects

Summary

Designed and simulated an AM superheterodyne radio receiver in LTspice as a personal project, covering the full signal chain from RF amplification through mixing, intermediate-frequency (IF) filtering, envelope detection and audio filtering. The receiver was designed with a later PCB implementation in mind. In simulation it picks out one station from several simultaneous AM signals and recovers its audio.

  • LtSpice
  • Analogue Design
  • RF Circuits
  • Signal Processing



What it does

  • Receives a combined input of multiple amplitude-modulated stations (carriers at 540, 576, 630 and 974 kHz, each with its own audio tone).
  • Selects the 576 kHz station, rejects the others, and recovers its audio tone.


    • What I learned

  • Why superheterodyne receivers use a fixed intermediate frequency: the hard-to-build selective filtering is done once at a single frequency instead of across the whole band.
  • The architecture of a superheterodyne receiver, and how each stage contributes to selectivity.
  • Designing and tuning LC resonant circuits and BJT amplifier stages.

    • Limitations

  • The design is tuned to a single frequency. Tuning across the band would need variable (ganged) capacitors in the RF stage and local oscillator, so the IF stays fixed at 455 kHz while the receiver tunes.
  • The mixer and buffering are semi-analogue: the local oscillator is an ideal pulse source, and the stages are isolated with behavioural voltage sources.
